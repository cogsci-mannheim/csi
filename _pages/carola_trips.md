I am a historical linguist by training and have extensively worked on contact-induced change in medieval times in Britain. Coming from a background in generative grammar I have always had an interest in understanding how language is acquired, what the necessary conditions are for a language to be learnt and what the cognitive abilities are that make this possible. In my research (child) language acquisition is the driving force in language change and I subscribe to Charles Yang's Tolerance Principle to account for when rules generalise and when they become exceptions, back in the history of English as well as in contemporary English. Methodologically, I work with annotated corpora and I compare historical data with CHILDES data. In the future, I'd like to gain a more comprehensive picture of human cognition, the role of language competence, and why it is important to engage with human-centered AI.
